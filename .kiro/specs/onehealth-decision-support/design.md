# Design: Sentinel-52 One Health decision support

## The One Health system map

Required by Task 1. The map is a deliverable in its own right and should be drawn as a figure for the
report, but the structure it encodes is this:

```
ENVIRONMENT            ANIMAL / PRODUCTION           FOOD                 HUMAN
-----------            --------------------          ----                 -----
NOAA climate     -->   establishment process   -->   lot in the     -->   illness burden
(temp, precip,         control (FSIS sampling,       food bank's          by serotype
 season)               serotype, WGS resistance)     inventory            (NORS)
                              |                          |                     |
                              v                          v                     |
                       FSIS category,              perishability,              |
                       recalls, alerts             quantity, destination       |
                                                         |                     |
                                                         v                     |
                                            POPULATION VULNERABILITY <---------+
                                            (SVI, food access) shapes
                                            who bears the consequence
```

Two arrows matter more than the rest. **Climate to production** is why risk is seasonal rather than
constant. **Human burden back to population vulnerability** is why the same contamination event is
not equally costly everywhere, and it is the link that turns this from a safety tool into a One
Health tool.

The arrow deliberately **not** drawn is establishment to illness. The FSIS licence forbids it and no
code may construct it. Serotype burden enters as a property of a serotype, never of a plant.

## Architecture

Unchanged in shape from the previous spec, extended in inputs. Static React, built by Vite, deployed
to GitHub Pages. Everything precomputed in Python.

```
analysis/scripts/
  01-08   existing FSIS pipeline (validated, unchanged)
  09      NORS fetch, public CDC endpoint                     [done]
  10      serotype burden weighting                           [done]
  11      NOAA/NCEI climate fetch                             [new]
  12      FSIS recalls and public health alerts fetch         [new]
  13      SVI and USDA food-access fetch                      [new]
  14      synthetic food-bank inventory generator             [new]
  15      integrated risk model: calibration + ablation       [new]
  16      decision layer: actions, scenarios, equity, loss    [new]
  17      app data build -> app/public/data/*.json            [new]
```

### The honest problem: there is no real inventory

A food bank's lot-level inventory is not public data and we have none. Task 1 permits "actor-specific
operational data" but we cannot obtain it inside a week.

**So the inventory layer is synthetic, and it is labelled synthetic everywhere it appears**: in the
app UI, in the data dictionary, in provenance, and in the report. It is generated by
`14_build_synthetic_inventory.py` from a stated, seeded procedure: real establishments drawn from the
FSIS poultry population, plausible lot sizes, plausible perishability windows, destinations drawn
from real county service areas.

What this means for the claims: every statement about *risk* rests on real data. Every statement
about *pounds* rests on a simulation whose assumptions are in the open. The report must not blur
that line, and the app must not either. A judge who thinks our inventory is real would be misled, and
that is on us to prevent.

## Data layer

| Source | Script | Role | Required by |
|---|---|---|---|
| FSIS establishment sampling, FY2014-2026 | 01-02 | process control, categories, serotypes, WGS resistance | brief: required |
| FSIS recalls and public health alerts | 12 | hard events that outrank model scores | brief: required |
| CDC NORS, public endpoint | 09-10 | human illness burden by serotype | brief: substitute for PulseNet |
| NOAA/NCEI Climate Data Online | 11 | seasonality, heat exposure | brief: required, needs free token |
| CDC/ATSDR SVI | 13 | population vulnerability | team-added, vulnerability |
| USDA food access data | 13 | food-security context of service areas | team-added, food security |
| Synthetic inventory | 14 | lots, quantity, perishability, destination | **simulated, labelled** |

The NOAA token is per-user and must not be committed. `11_fetch_noaa.py` reads it from the
`NOAA_CDO_TOKEN` environment variable and fails with a clear message naming the variable if absent.

### Why climate is in the model rather than decoration

The existing pipeline already shows a month-of-year range in routine positivity. If climate adds
nothing beyond month-of-year, the ablation will say so, and that is a legitimate result to report
rather than a failure to hide. Including a required dataset and reporting that its marginal value is
small is honest; including it and implying it helped is not.

## Model layer

The existing early-warning work carries over and is the backbone of Task 2.

- **Transparent baseline:** binomial projection, AUROC 0.917, CI 0.903 to 0.931. Stays the default.
- **Integrated model:** gradient boosting, AUROC 0.926, CI 0.913 to 0.938, now extended with climate,
  serotype burden, recall history and vulnerability features.
- **Rule-based policy baseline:** the current Category 2 flag. 23.6 percent of units, 21.9 percent
  precision, 81.3 percent recall.

### What must be added

**Calibration.** Partly present and explicitly required. Brier is already computed per scorer and a
decile reliability table exists for gradient boosting, tracking closely at the top decile (0.432
predicted against 0.426 observed). What is missing is the same table for the **binomial projection**,
which is the default scorer and therefore the one that has to be calibrated, plus a reliability figure
for the report. A score used to hold food must be calibrated, not merely well-ranked: "30 percent"
has to mean something to the person deciding.

**Ablation.** One row per added source, dropped singly from the full model, reporting the change in
AUROC and average precision with establishment-clustered intervals. This satisfies both Task 2's
marginal-value requirement and Task 5's team-science ablation.

Sources whose contribution is within noise are reported as such and stay in the table.

## Decision layer

Converting a score into one of four actions, under constraints.

```
score + interval
  + lot perishability, quantity
  + destination vulnerability (SVI) and food access
  + recall or alert status
  + review capacity this period
      |
      v
   action: distribute now | prioritize | hold for review | divert
      |
      +-- high-consequence (hold, divert) -> human confirmation required
      +-- cost stated in pounds and in communities affected
      +-- counterfactual action shown with its own cost
```

**Objective.** Not a single scalar. The scenario builder exposes a frontier across three quantities
that genuinely conflict: risk caught, food distributed, and distribution reaching high-need areas.
Collapsing those into one number would hide exactly the trade-off the brief asks us to surface.

**Equity check.** For any policy, compare hold and divert rates across SVI quartiles of destination
service area. Report the disparity whichever direction it runs, including no disparity.

## Application

Five components, named as the brief names them.

```
App
├── DataProvider              fetch + cache precomputed JSON, named load errors
├── Layout                    nav, theme, provenance footer, synthetic-data banner
├── CurrentRiskView           R1
├── AlertQueue                R2  recalls pinned above model scores
├── ScenarioBuilder           R3  three named scenarios + free exploration
├── RecommendedActionPanel    R4  one action, its cost, its counterfactual
├── EvidencePanel             R5  signals, interval, calibration, sources
├── EquityView                R6  disparity check across SVI quartiles
├── MethodsPage               caveats, FSIS restriction, synthetic-data statement
└── shared/
    ├── Chart                 one y-axis, legend, paired TableView
    ├── SyntheticBadge        on every figure derived from simulated inventory
    ├── SourceNote            filename + generated_at
    └── ConfirmHighConsequence  blocking confirmation for hold and divert
```

`SyntheticBadge` is not decoration. It is the mechanism that keeps the simulated inventory from being
read as real, and it belongs on every pounds-denominated number in the interface.

### State

Threshold, review capacity and food-security weight are application-level, shared by the scenario
builder and the queue. Filters are page-local.

### Routing and deployment

Hash routing, `base: '/sentinel-52/'`. The Pages workflow at `docs/pages-workflow-for-later.yml`
moves into `.github/workflows/` only once `npm run build` succeeds locally.

## Testing

- **Contract test:** every `app/public/data/*.json` has the keys its components read. This is the one
  that catches a pipeline change breaking the app.
- **Guardrail tests, which are correctness not style:**
  - no burden or decision output contains an establishment identifier joined to illness data
  - no code path emits the strings "safe" or "unsafe" as a verdict on a lot or establishment
  - every pounds-denominated figure carries the synthetic flag
  - hold and divert cannot be issued without the confirmation step
- **Unit:** ranking order, threshold-to-queue-length arithmetic, equity quartile assignment.
- **Render:** each page against committed fixtures.

## What changed from the previous spec

`.kiro/specs/watchlist-app/` was written on 2026-10-26 before the brief existed, from the track title
alone. It aimed at a regulator allocating inspection capacity. It is kept in the repository rather
than deleted, because the brief asks us to disclose how the work evolved and a tidied history would
misrepresent that.

Its model, validation and evidence requirements survive almost intact. Its user, its actions and its
objective do not.
